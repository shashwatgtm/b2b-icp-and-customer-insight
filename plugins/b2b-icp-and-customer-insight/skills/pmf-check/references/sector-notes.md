# Sector notes for the nine verticals (carried inside this skill so it stands alone)

Use these to word questions, hypotheses and checks in the buyer's own language. They hold no statistic, benchmark, market size or fact about a named company: every company fact comes from the user or a page fetched in the session, and every figure is the user's or labelled hypothetical. A row is a starting point to test, never a finding.

| Vertical | Who usually champions, signs and evaluates | Objections buyers often raise | Measures buyers in this vertical use | What proof they trust |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Logistics tech | operations, transport, supply chain or warehouse heads champion; the CFO or COO signs for larger contracts; IT and the operations team on the floor evaluate | integration with the existing TMS, WMS or ERP; disruption during peak season; adoption by drivers or floor staff | on time delivery, cost per shipment or per drop, vehicle or dock utilisation, order cycle time | a pilot on the buyer's own lanes or sites, references from similar networks |
| Fintech | the business owner signs: for finance software sold to finance teams, the CFO or finance head; for a payments, banking or lending product, the chief executive, product head, CTO or head of payments, and in lending the chief risk officer or head of credit; the product or operations lead champions; engineering checks the integration (often in a sandbox); risk, compliance and information security review controls, data handling and the audit trail | regulatory fit, data residency, security review, integration with core banking or ERP; we already have a provider for this; reliability and what happens to work in flight during an outage; can it explain a decline | days sales outstanding, cost of funds, reconciliation time, approval turnaround, loss or default rates, time to go live, uptime, payment success rate, settlement time, days to close the books, time to decision | regulator approvals and licences the vendor can show, security certifications, a reference in a similar institution; a before and after on one flow from the buyer's own data with the period named; for lending, a back test on the buyer's own past applications, labelled as back tested |
| SaaS | a functional head (sales, marketing, support, HR) champions; the budget owner or CFO signs; IT, security and admins evaluate | switching cost from the current tool, adoption, integrations, price per seat as the team grows | adoption and active use, time saved, pipeline or productivity measures of the function | a trial or pilot with the buyer's own data, peer reviews, references in the same function |
| Vertical SaaS | the operator of the industry served (for example a field service or plant head) champions; the owner or CFO signs; IT and the daily users evaluate | fit with the industry's own workflow, offline or field use, migration from spreadsheets or a legacy system | the industry's own operating measures (jobs per day, first time fix, uptime) | references from the same industry, a walkthrough of the buyer's actual workflow |
| AI native companies: AI applications and agents | the owner of the process the AI changes signs; the business lead who feels the problem champions; the data or AI lead evaluates on the buyer's own data; security, risk and compliance and legal counsel review where data goes and how decisions are explained | accuracy on the buyer's own data, data privacy and model use of customer data, cost at scale, explainability; it will not fit our existing systems; is the vendor stable enough; can we trust an agent to act on its own | task accuracy and time saved on the buyer's own cases, cost per task or per case, how often a person has to step in, time to a working pilot, turnaround time per case | an evaluation or pilot on the buyer's data with the method stated, scored against the way the work is judged today; security and data handling documents |
| AI native companies: AI infrastructure and platform buyers (model and voice APIs, model serving, GPU compute, ML platforms) | the CTO, VP engineering or head of data and AI signs; the engineering, machine learning or ML platform lead who builds on it champions and runs the evaluation; security, legal and procurement review data handling, region and usage terms; finance checks committed spend (to test: who owns compute spend at the buyer, engineering or finance) | quality on our own data or workload; latency and reliability at our scale; usage cost grows with volume; our data cannot be used for training or leave our region; capacity when we need it | response latency, uptime of the service, cost per request or per unit of compute, requests handled at peak, time to integrate | a side by side evaluation on the buyer's own workload against the current provider, with quality, latency and cost per unit reported together |
| ITeS (IT and IT enabled services) | delivery, engineering or a capability centre head champions; the CIO, CTO or procurement signs through a proposal; procurement and vendor management evaluate | delivery risk, team continuity, price against other vendors, transition risk | delivery quality, time to staff, cost per outcome, service levels met | case studies from similar engagements, certifications, references, the proposed team |
| Telecom: enterprise buyers of connectivity and communications | the CIO, CTO or the head of the function using the service signs; network, infrastructure or product leads champion; engineers integrate; security and compliance review; procurement compares rate cards; finance checks cost per unit of service | price against the current supplier (compare total cost of the outcome, not the rate card); outage risk during the switch; integration with systems already run; regulatory and data protection review | uptime, mean time to repair, latency, incidents per month, service credits paid, cost per site or per connection, time to go live | a pilot on a few sites or routes, compared with the incumbent's record for the same sites over the same period |
| Telecom: operators (network equipment, network or billing software sold to telcos) | network, operations, product, wholesale or revenue assurance heads champion; the CTO or CFO signs; network engineering evaluates | interoperability with the existing network, standards compliance, field reliability, long qualification cycles | network uptime, coverage, latency, cost per site, revenue leakage found, settlement cycle time | lab and field trials, standards certifications, operator references; for billing or assurance software, a shadow run on the operator's own records signed off by finance |
| Software companies | engineering, product or platform leads champion; the CTO or VP engineering signs; developers and security reviewers evaluate | fit with the stack, open source alternatives, lock in, security review | developer time saved, release frequency, incidents, time to resolve | documentation quality, a hands on trial, community or peer use, security review results |
| Cybersecurity | security operations or a CISO champions; the CISO or CIO signs, with the CFO for larger spends; security engineers, risk and compliance evaluate | alert overload, overlap with tools already owned, proof of detection, audit evidence | time to detect and respond, audit findings closed, coverage of assets, false positives handled | a proof of concept in the buyer's environment, certifications, references; never fear selling and never a named breach victim without a public source |

More words by vertical, to test (roles, objections and measure names only; no figure):
- Logistics tech: planners and operations staff use it daily; finance checks the cost case. Objections: we already have a system for this; the cost is hard to justify on our margins; online sellers may raise cash on delivery money arriving late, or surprise weight or return charges. Measures: exception rate, delay and damage claims, manual planning time.
- SaaS: objections: we already have a tool for this (compare one real team over one real week); security and IT review. Measures: time to value, renewal rate, expansion revenue, support load, seats in active use. Support software: the head of support signs; first response and resolution time. CRM and marketing: the revenue or sales head signs, revenue operations champions; pipeline created, forecast accuracy. Billing: finance led; billing errors and disputes, failed payments recovered.
- Vertical SaaS: the owner, managing director or head of operations signs; the department head who feels the daily problem champions; an implementation lead runs the rollout. Objections: we run on spreadsheets and it works; it will not fit how our trade works; moving our data. Measures: adoption by role, hours of admin saved, errors and rework, time to first value.
- ITeS: the COO or a business unit head can also sign; finance checks rates and total cost; security and legal check access and contract terms. Objections: a cheaper vendor (compare total cost of the outcome); attrition and dependence on key people; lock in. Measures: milestones met on time, rework, team stability, transition milestones met. Sales motion: RFP or relationship led, often after a small pilot or paid discovery. BPO: average handle time, first contact resolution, agent attrition.
- Software companies: a platform engineering lead, head of DevOps or engineering manager champions; a security lead reviews code and data access. Objections: per seat or usage cost at our scale; migrating what we have. Measures: lead time for changes, build time, mean time to recovery, incidents after a release.
- Cybersecurity: a security architect and the head of risk and compliance review the evidence; IT operations deploy it. Objections: integration with our existing security tools; we need proof before budget (a fixed length proof of value with success criteria agreed in writing first). Measures: critical exposures open, alerts per analyst, time to prepare an audit. Proof: real findings from the proof of value on the buyer's own environment and the time it took to act on them.

How to use this table:
- Read the vertical from the user's own words about their product and buyer. For an AI company, choose between the applications row and the infrastructure and platform row by what it sells and to whom (a GPU cloud or model API sold to engineering teams is infrastructure; an agent sold to a finance team is an application). For telecom, choose the enterprise or operator row by who buys. For software sold to a business, choose by who buys and uses it: a functional head (sales, support, HR, finance) is SaaS; the operator of one trade or industry is vertical SaaS; an engineering, platform or developer lead is software companies. If a company calls itself one thing and sells another (for example "AI native" field service software), the product and buyer decide the row, and the skill says which row it used and why.
- A services firm has engagements, proposals and delivery teams, not a free trial or an activation moment. A network equipment maker sells through trials and certification, not seats.
- Regions served by these notes: India, Middle East and Africa, Southeast Asia, United Kingdom, United States, Western Europe; stages pre Series A to Series C. For any other sector, work only from what the user typed and pages fetched in the session.
