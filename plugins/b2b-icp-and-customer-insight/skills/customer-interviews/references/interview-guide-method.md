# Interview guide method

## The arc (a suggested shape; the user decides the length)
1. Opening: who you are, why you asked, how long, permission to take notes, that there are no right answers.
2. Context: their role, their team, how the job is done today, in their words.
3. Core: the questions that test the hypotheses, asked about past events ("Tell me about the last time ..."), not opinions about the future.
4. Probes: "What happened next?", "How did you decide?", "What did that cost you?", "Who else was involved?", then silence: let the answer finish.
5. Closing: anything we should have asked, who else to speak to, permission to follow up.

## Question rules
- Open questions about past behaviour; one question at a time.
- Never name the product's feature in the question; never lead ("Wouldn't it be great if ...").
- Ask for the last real example, the workaround in use, and what it costs (time, money, risk), in the interviewee's own measures.
- Ask who else was involved, to learn the buying roles without asking for names.
- The interviewer never pitches during a research interview.

## The hypothesis table (one row per hypothesis the team gives, quoted whole)

| Hypothesis (the team's words) | Question that could confirm it | Question that tests how severe it is | Question that looks for counter evidence | What would count as support | What would count against |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The counter evidence question must not presume the hypothesis or any factor in it. It names neither the factor the team believes in nor an "else" or "apart from" that treats that factor as given; it asks openly about what happened and lets the interviewee name the causes. Weak: "Apart from the documents, what else affected whether you went ahead?" (presumes the documents mattered). Better: "Walk me through what decided whether you went ahead, in the order it happened." Weak: "What else, if anything, slowed the purchase besides security review?" Better: "What affected the timing of the last purchase, from first conversation to signature?"

When you explain why a hypothesis may not hold, do not state a general claim about buyers, roles or a region as fact ("many merchants are family run"); turn the doubt into a question for the guide, or cite the sector notes row by name and mark it to test. This includes how a role will answer you ("a finance head is unlikely to call themselves the blocker"): state the design choice instead ("so the guide does not rest on one role's account of its own part, ask both roles to walk through the last decision").

Who the interviewees are (customers, prospects, lost deals, past users) comes only from the user. When a hypothesis can only be tested with people who already use the product (for example how trust in its output grows), and the user has not said whether any do, write the questions for each case and ask; never state that none or all of them use it.

A hypothesis that is a claim about the market or the company (for example a figure from a website) is not put to the interviewee; it is set aside as a fact to source.

## Question sets by interview type
- **Discovery** (do they have the problem?): how the job is done today, the last time it went wrong, the workaround, who owns it, what has been tried.
- **Validation** (is our solution the right answer?): walk through their current process, where our approach would fit, what would stop them adopting it, what they would need to see. No demo during the questions.
- **Churn research** (why customers left): what they hoped for at the start, when value stopped, what they use now, what would have kept them. Research only; saving a live account is renewal-save-plan in b2b-customer-success.
- **Win and loss research** (why buyers chose): the trigger, the options considered, how they decided, who decided, what nearly changed their mind. Competitor battle cards are competitive-intelligence in b2b-sales-enablement.
- **Persona research** (how a role works): goals, measures they are judged on, a typical week, tools, who they report to, what they read.
- **Case study interviews** are not run here: evidence-research-asset in the research plugin.

## Next round sample (arithmetic by script)
When the plan names groups to interview (for example paying and free teams, or wins and losses), put each group's interviews done and planned in "next_round" (references/synthesis-method.md, section 1a) and copy the script's line per group: done + planned = total, and how many short of the minimum per group. Never write "at least five per group" or any total the numbers do not add up to. The minimum is the user's; if none is given, the script uses 5, its own line for a direction, and says it is not a research standard.

## Timing (suggested, not a rule)
The guide fits the length the user gives. If the user gives none, it asks once or marks a core set of questions and an optional set, so the interviewer can cut the optional set if time runs short. No length is presented as a norm.

## Sector words
Use references/sector-notes.md for the interviewee's vertical: their measures, objections and proof words. A services buyer has engagements and proposals; a network buyer has trials and certification.
